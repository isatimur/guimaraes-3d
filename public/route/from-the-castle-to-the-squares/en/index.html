From the castle to the squares

Five places, from the castle hill down to the squares of the old town.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Guimarães Castle

    Stop 1 of 5 · 10th century

    Countess Mumadona Dias founded the castle around 958 to shield her monastery from Moorish and Norse raids.

    From the 15th century artillery made the castle useless.

    In 1836 the town council came within one vote of demolishing it for paving stone.

    In the real city

    Between the castle and the palace stands the Romanesque church of São Miguel, and the statue of Afonso Henriques stands by the palace ramp.

  2. Church of São Miguel do Castelo

    Stop 2 of 5 · 1239

    The church is first mentioned in 1216, in a document of the collegiate of Guimarães.

    Archbishop Silvestre Godinho consecrated the church around 1239.

    Legend holds that the first King of Portugal was baptised in its font, although the church is a full century younger than the king.

    In the real city

    It is dim inside: let your eyes adjust, then look at the floor slabs and the font to the left of the door.

    Sources

    • Igreja de São Miguel do Castelo — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Church of São Miguel do Castelo — Wikipedia (en)

  3. Palace of the Dukes of Braganza

    Stop 3 of 5 · 15th century

    The palace was commissioned by Afonso, illegitimate son of King John I and 8th Count of Barcelos.

    His heirs preferred Vila Viçosa in the south, and the palace emptied.

    In 1611 the Poor Clares and in 1666 the Capuchins were allowed to take stone from the palace for their convents.

    In the real city

    The statue of Afonso Henriques stands by the ramp in front, and the castle is a three-minute walk through the park.

  4. Church of Our Lady of Oliveira

    Stop 4 of 5 · 1387

    Around 949 Countess Mumadona Dias founded a house on the low ground of Vimaranes, dedicated to the Saviour, the Virgin and the Apostles; at first it was a double monastery for monks and nuns.

    Around 1513 a battlemented Manueline tower was raised over the church.

    In the square in front stands the Gothic canopy of the Padrão do Salado, raised in memory of the battle of 1340, and beside it grows an olive tree, the symbol of the miracle of 1342 that gave the church its name.

    In the real city

    From Largo da Oliveira, the Gothic arcade of the old town hall leads straight into Praça de Santiago.

  5. Santiago Square

    Stop 5 of 5 · medieval

    Documents mention the square over many centuries, and it keeps its medieval layout.

    The chapel of Saint James was demolished in January 1887; its plan is marked in the paving.

    The houses with wooden balconies that look so romantic today were once thought slums.

    In the real city

    Look for the line in the paving that traces the plan of the demolished chapel.
