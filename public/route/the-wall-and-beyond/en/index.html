The wall and the town beyond it

Five places along the medieval wall and beyond it: its one surviving tower, the square and the tanners' quarter that grew up outside it, a Franciscan convent and the church on the old fairground.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Largo do Toural

    Stop 1 of 5 · 18th–21st centuries

    In 1791 the town council leased a plot along the wall for building.

    The façades gained arcades at ground level, huge windows two storeys high, dormer roofs and fine wrought-iron balconies — the look still known today as the city's “drawing room”.

    In 2011 the 16th-century Renaissance fountain returned to the square, and in 2012, for the European Capital of Culture, the whole space was remodelled and linked to Avenida Alberto Sampaio.

    In the real city

    It is a handy place to start a walk: south to the Alfândega tower inscribed “Aqui nasceu Portugal” and on through the arcade to the squares of Santiago and Oliveira, or north to the Dukes' Palace and the castle.

  2. City Wall and Alfândega Tower (“Aqui nasceu Portugal”)

    Stop 2 of 5 · 13th–14th centuries

    João I ordered the inner wall between the two burghs demolished, making them one town.

    The Torre da Alfândega is the only surviving tower of Guimarães' medieval wall.

    It bears the inscription “Aqui nasceu Portugal”.

    In the real city

    Start at Largo da Condessa Mumadona and walk along Avenida Alberto Sampaio: from the wall you can see the Dukes' Palace, the castle and Penha.

  3. Couros Zone

    Stop 3 of 5 · 13th–20th centuries

    Leather working in Guimarães is documented from the 13th century.

    In the 19th century the area we now call Couros became a privileged centre of hide processing, its output still based on traditional technique and hand labour.

    In July 1977 the Couros Zone was listed as an Imóvel de Interesse Público.

    In the real city

    Head west from the centre along Rua de Couros, following the stream: the tanning vats and old factories lie on both sides of the street.

  4. Church of Saint Francis

    Stop 4 of 5 · 1400–18th century

    The Franciscans reached Guimarães in 1217 with Friar Gualter, sent by Francis of Assisi.

    The present convent was rebuilt from 1400 by order of João I; the Gothic apse was finished around 1461.

    Constança de Noronha, first Duchess of Bragança, is buried in the church (tomb with recumbent effigy).

    In the real city

    Look at the Gothic apse from outside, the main portal, the Baroque hall interior and the cloister with its chapter house.

  5. Church of Our Lady of Consolation and the Holy Steps

    Stop 5 of 5 · 1769–1785

    The new church was commissioned in 1769 and is probably the last work designed by André Soares (1720–1769).

    It was finished in October 1785; the twin towers, staircase and balustrade were added in the 19th century.

    The Oratórios (Passos) form a Via Sacra with painted wooden figures of the Passion.

    In the real city

    The church stands on Largo da República do Brasil, in front of a neat garden, and from here it is easy to walk on to the lower station of the Penha cable car.
